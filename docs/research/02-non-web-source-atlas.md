# Source Atlas

## 1. Plasticity

Clay is not merely “soft.”

Its important behavior is that under suitable conditions it can deform under force without immediately breaking and can retain a new form.

### Source relationship

**force → deformation → retained form**

This is more meaningful than generic softness.

Do not yet translate this into UI.

---

## 2. Material Memory

Clay can retain evidence of previous stress and particle orientation.

During later drying or firing, earlier manipulation may reappear through deformation or warping.

### Source idea

> The material can remember what happened to it.

This introduces a temporal property:

**past interaction → latent state → later consequence**

---

## 3. Centering

On a pottery wheel, rotation alone is not enough.

Before pulling a stable form, the clay must become properly centered around the rotational axis.

A visible vessel depends on an invisible condition established first.

### Source idea

**stability before expression**

or

**form begins before form becomes visible**

---

## 4. Rotation

Rotation provides continuous energy.

But maximum speed is not the goal.

Too much speed can reduce control; too little can make forming difficult.

### Source tension

**energy × restraint**

**movement × control**

The interesting property is not “things spin.”

It is controlled continuous energy.

---

## 5. Pressure / Counter-pressure

Wheel throwing depends on pressure, resistance, support, release, and repeated correction.

A form emerges from forces acting against one another.

### Source loop

**pressure → material response → correction → pressure**

This is a feedback system, not a one-click transformation.

---

## 6. Pinching

Pinching forms clay through direct repeated compression by the fingers.

The method is local, tactile, incremental, and visibly tied to the hand.

### Source property

**small repeated intervention produces global form**

---

## 7. Coiling

Coil building constructs vessels through accumulated rings or lengths of clay.

Unlike radial wheel formation, the form is built vertically and incrementally.

### Source property

**layer → layer → layer → whole**

This gives us a formation model fundamentally different from wheel throwing.

---

## 8. Two Formation Logics

The research now contains at least two distinct physical grammars:

### Radial formation
A centered mass develops through rotational force.

### Accumulated formation
A vessel develops through repeated stacked material.

This distinction matters.

“Pottery” does not imply one universal motion language.

---

## 9. Drying

Drying is active transformation despite low visible motion.

Water loss produces shrinkage.

Uneven drying can generate internal stress, warping, or cracking.

### Source idea

> Time can alter form even when nothing appears to move.

### Source relationship

**uneven change → stored stress → visible consequence**

---

## 10. Shrinkage

Loss is not neutral.

As water leaves clay, dimensions change.

Different regions can change at different rates.

### Source idea

**subtraction can reshape the whole**

---

## 11. Warping

Warping can be the later visible outcome of conditions established earlier:

- uneven thickness,
- uneven drying,
- particle orientation,
- stored stress,
- geometry,
- support conditions.

### Source idea

**cause and effect can be separated in time**

---

## 12. Firing

Firing is not a finishing filter.

It changes the material state.

At important stages of heating, the clay undergoes chemical and structural transformation and eventually loses its original plastic reversibility.

### Source threshold

**before firing:** reshape / revise / return  
**after transformation:** committed form

### Source idea

> At some point, the object commits to what it has become.

This is an irreversible threshold.

---

## 13. Vitrification

At higher temperatures, ceramic material can progressively densify and develop glass-like bonding.

The important source property is not visual shine.

It is **state transition**.

### Source relationship

**same object → materially different state**

---

## 14. Glaze

Glaze is not simply paint added after the object is complete.

During firing, glaze materials melt, react, and form a glassy surface bonded to the ceramic body.

### Source idea

**surface can emerge from transformation, not decoration**

---

## 15. Crazing

Fine crack networks in glaze can emerge from differences in thermal expansion between glaze and ceramic body.

The visible pattern is evidence of a relationship between two material systems.

### Source idea

**mismatch → tension → pattern**

Important guardrail:

Do not romanticize every defect as craft.

The value here is causal trace.

---

## 16. Trace, Not “Imperfection”

The research corrects an early shorthand.

Handmade does **not** mean the maker is trying to produce mistakes.

Ceramic craft contains enormous effort toward:

- centering,
- controlling thickness,
- trimming,
- managing moisture,
- managing heat,
- avoiding structural failure,
- and producing usable objects.

The stronger concept is:

### TRACE

A trace is evidence of:

- hand,
- tool,
- support,
- heat,
- pressure,
- process,
- material behavior,
- or time.

Trace can exist without failure.

# Egyptian Material Research

## 17. Nile Silt and Early Egyptian Pottery

Predynastic Egyptian pottery includes vessels made with Nile-silt clays and techniques such as:

- coiling,
- hand smoothing,
- slips,
- burnishing,
- controlled surface coloration,
- black-topped treatments.

This gives the project a potential Egyptian source layer grounded in material practice rather than visual cliché.

---

## 18. Red Slip / Burnishing / Black-Topped Surfaces

Historic Egyptian ceramic surfaces demonstrate that an “Egyptian material language” can emerge through:

- clay body,
- finishing,
- polishing,
- firing behavior,
- contrast,
- and process.

It does not require applied symbols.

---

## 19. Egyptian Faience

Egyptian faience adds another important material model.

Its color and glossy surface are produced through chemistry, firing, mineral content, and flux behavior rather than simply painted representation.

### Source idea

**color can be a material event**

not merely graphic styling.

---

## 20. Adaptation Rather Than Frozen Heritage

Egyptian pottery history also contains adaptation of imported forms or decorative influences into local production and local vessel logic.

This matters because cultural authenticity does not require freezing design in a historical period.

### Source relationship

**encounter → absorb → transform → new local form**

This is highly compatible with the experimental workflow itself.

---

# Source Tensions Discovered

The source world now contains multiple useful tensions:

- **soft ↔ fired**
- **wet ↔ dry**
- **reversible ↔ irreversible**
- **centered ↔ warped**
- **pressure ↔ release**
- **raw ↔ glazed**
- **matte ↔ glass-like**
- **control ↔ material agency**
- **rotation ↔ accumulation**
- **visible action ↔ latent consequence**
- **repetition ↔ trace**
- **formation ↔ function**

These are not design directions yet.

They are the raw conceptual field.

# What We Deliberately Did Not Decide

Step 02 does **not** choose:

- colors,
- typography,
- layout,
- hero structure,
- navigation,
- animation technique,
- WebGL usage,
- interaction effects,
- page transitions,
- or final visual motifs.

A source is not automatically a design instruction.

Example:

**rotation exists in the craft**  
does not mean  
**the website should rotate everything.**

The next step must reduce this atlas aggressively before any digital translation.
